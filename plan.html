Sauces

  • For the tuna pasta — Molho nata Mimosa para massas (€1.09). A ready-made cream sauce for pasta, no tomato. Warm it through with a little alho and azeite, then fold in the tuna and the cooked esparguete.
  • For the pork — Molho nata Mimosa para carne (€1.09). The Portuguese answer to gravy: a cream sauce made for meat, poured over the one-pot stew. If you'd rather make it from scratch, use the pan juices with a spoon of farinha.
  • Cream sauce (natas) — plain nata culinária, for the risotto, the creme de legumes and the bacalhau com natas.
  • Béchamel — made at home: farinha whisked into warm leite with manteiga.
  • Tomato sauce — homemade from tomate triturado, onion and azeite. For the tomato soup and the tomato rice only.

How the week is built

  • No onion in the pasta. Onion appears only as a frying base — softened first in the rice, the stew and the soups. Neither massa dish contains onion.
  • Tuna pasta gets a cream sauce, never tomato — the tuna is only ever paired with oil, garlic or molho de natas.
  • Soups are only tomato, vegetables or carrot. No bean, kale or mixed soups: sopa de legumes, sopa de tomate, creme de legumes and creme de cenoura.
  • No milk as a drink. Breakfast is served with iogurte líquido; lunch and dinner come with água, and three meals get a refrigerante. Milk appears only as an ingredient — in the cereal, the béchamel and the creme de legumes.
  • Water is tap water, so it is free and not in the shopping list; the only drinks you buy are the iogurte líquido and one refrigerante.
  • Breakfasts stay light — only three of seven use bread (two tostas and a sandes); the rest are iogurte with muesli or cereal with milk and fruit.

How the prices work

  • Each meal's price is its share of the shopping list: every purchase is divided across the meals that use it, so the 21 meals and 7 snacks add up to the €63.38 food total.
  • Delivery (€1.49) is not included in the meal prices; it's on the order total instead.
  • Meal prices are rounded to the cent, so adding all 28 of them gives €63.36 — a two-cent rounding drift, not a costing error.
  • Every line was checked against the live catalogue, cheapest first. Where a cheaper item exists but was not taken, it's noted below.
  • Prices are live from the Glovo Auchan catalogue and can change; fresh produce and meat are sold by weight, so the final bill may differ by a few cents.
  • The catalogue has no plain milk, so lactose-free milk is used. Your ticks are saved in this browser.

Price audit

  • The cream (natas) in the list is the cheapest one: Nata UHT Auchan para culinária, 200 ml, €0.79. The Mimosa cream is €0.89. The two Mimosa lines you spotted are a different product — the ready-made cream sauces for pasta and meat (€1.09 each), and €1.09 is the cheapest of that type in the catalogue. A plain Molho Béchamel Auchan at €0.89 is cheaper still, but it's a béchamel, not a cream sauce for pasta.
  • Fixed this pass — three lines were not the cheapest: pasta switched from Esparguete 500 g (€0.69) to Massa Penne Auchan 500 g (€0.65); the sardines switched from 2 × Pescador 54 g (€1.72) to 1 × Sardinha Auchan em tomate 120 g (€1.15), which is cheaper and gives more fish; and the greens switched from Esparregado ultracongelado 350 g (€2.59) to Grelos Bonduelle 400 g (€2.09). Together that saves €1.15.
  • Deliberately not the cheapest, and why: Maria biscuits €0.74/200 g rather than the €0.49 cracker (0.37 vs 0.39 €/100 g — cheaper by weight, and the Portuguese staple); the 4×125 g yogurt at €0.73 rather than Skyr 150 g at €0.65 (0.15 vs 0.43 €/100 g); queijo flamengo ralado €1.09 rather than queijo fresco €0.79, because flamengo is what melts on the toasts; chicken pieces €2.24/kg rather than chicken livers €1.12, which don't work in arroz de frango; and tomate triturado €0.69/400 g rather than polpa €0.50/210 g, cheaper by weight.
